--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FREQUENCY AND LATENCY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The model is stateless and recomputes on every request - effectively per ball, if you
call it per ball.

End-to-end today, measured just now: 1.9-2.5s per call. That is honest and it is not
good. It is a single Railway instance with no CDN and an upstream fetch inside the
request; the model itself is a small fraction of it. If you feed us the ball state
directly, the upstream fetch disappears and that number drops a long way. I am not going
to quote you a figure for that until I have measured it.

Related and relevant to you: on our current third-party feed we measure upstream
staleness at p50 133s for the live miniscore. That is the feed, not us - and it is one
of the reasons your ball-by-ball data is more interesting to me than a revenue share.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. INPUTS THE MODEL REQUIRES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Must have, in rough order of how much the model degrades without them:
  - ball-by-ball during play (not post-match)
  - per-over runs and wickets
  - live striker strike rate and current bowler economy
  - innings, runs, wickets, overs
  - fall of wickets

Should have: toss winner and decision, venue name, number of overs (for reduced-over
games), partnership runs and balls.

Does not need: ball-tracking. No speed, swing or spin. We work from delivery outcomes
only, and I would rather say that than imply a depth of data we do not have.
